# Twelve focused days

Start dates are flexible; use Day 1 as your first actual session. Budget 90–120 minutes/day. Read explanations after attempting, then rewrite from memory. Revisit missed problems after 2–3 days. Reduce new problems if review quality drops.

| Day | New problems | Main focus |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | 01, 02, 03 | Complement lookup, counts, distinct days |
| 2 | 04, 05, 06 | Representation, grouping, monotonic pairs |
| 3 | 07, 08, 09 | Two-pointer invariants; retry Day 1 misses |
| 4 | 10, 11, 12 | In-place prefix and time events; retry Day 2 |
| 5 | 13, 14, 15 | Bounded domains and asymmetric inputs |
| 6 | 16, 17, 18 | Search boundaries, monotonic predicates, top-k |
| 7 | 19, 20, 21 | Heaps and recursive decomposition |
| 8 | 22, 23, 24 | Tree traversal and inherited bounds |
| 9 | 25, 26, 27 | Graph direction, BFS, cycles |
| 10 | 28, 29, 30 | Mixed reasoning and prefix sums |
| 11 | Mock 1 and Mock 2 | No execution during 35-minute sessions |
| 12 | Mock 3 + two weak-pattern retries | Clear explanation, manual debugging |

Daily allocation: 5 minutes recall; 60–90 minutes attempts; 20–25 minutes review/retry. These are training goals, not a guarantee of interview readiness. Prioritize a useful baseline and clean reasoning over forcing three completions.

Ready-enough checkpoint: on unfamiliar representative prompts, explain a baseline in 2–4 minutes, identify a plausible improvement, write coherent Python, dry-run edge cases, and justify complexity. Use mock evidence rather than problem count alone.
